Why this works better for quail

  • No elevated sleeping loft: the birds can use all 16 sq ft without navigating a ramp.
  • Protected retreat: a 2 × 2 ft sheltered corner offers cover from weather and stress, without reducing total accessible footprint.
  • Summer ventilation: two-plus hardware-cloth sides keep air moving; provide full-day shade in Atlanta.
  • Quail-friendly floor: solid removable tray holds dry pine shavings or other suitable litter—not permanently exposed wire beneath their feet.
  • Easy maintenance: two front doors open outward for feeding, cleaning, and egg collection.

Safety checks before building

  • Jump injuries: secure a soft, taut overhead barrier below the hard roof; prototype its height and tension so birds cannot entangle or strike hard framing.
  • Raccoons: use two-step, lockable latches and securely fastened mesh with screws and washers—not just staples.
  • Weather: keep one protected dry corner; shield wind-driven rain while preserving cross ventilation. Roof metal gets hot in direct sun.
  • Cleanup: make floor/tray removal possible without releasing birds; inspect drainage and structural loading.
  • Seasonal setup: move to deep shade in hot weather and use removable wind shields only when needed.
